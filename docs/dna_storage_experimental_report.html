1. Biological Motivation & Channel Realities

Synthetic deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) offers unprecedented archival density ($\approx 2.15 \times 10^{17}\text{ bytes/gram}$) and half-lives exceeding millennia. However, physical wet-lab synthesis (phosphoramidite or enzymatic printing) and high-throughput sequencing (Oxford Nanopore Technologies) do not obey classical additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) or binary symmetric channel (BSC) models. Instead, the channel is dominated by insertion-deletion (indel) bursts and enzymatic step dropouts where contiguous nucleotides are dropped without trace or index markers.

2. Mathematical Formulations & Biological Codec Architecture

2.1 Goldman Bijective Base-3 Quaternary Mapping (Nature 2013)

To ensure physical DNA strands can be synthesized without enzymatic stalling or Nanopore optical stutter, identical adjacent bases (homopolymers, e.g., AAAA or GGGG) are strictly forbidden. Rather than inefficient rejection sampling, we implement the bijective base-3 quaternary codec formulated by Goldman et al. (Nature 2013).

Let $\mathcal{B} = \{A, C, G, T\}$ denote the DNA alphabet. For any current nucleotide $B_{\text{prev}} \in \mathcal{B}$, the remaining three permissible bases are sorted in lexicographical order:

(Eq. 1) $$\mathcal{A}(B_{\text{prev}}) = \text{sort}\big(\mathcal{B} \setminus \{B_{\text{prev}}\}\big) = \big( \alpha_0, \alpha_1, \alpha_2 \big)$$

Each 8-bit byte $X \in \{0, \dots, 255\}$ is decomposed into $6$ ternary digits (trits) $t_k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ via radix-3 representation:

(Eq. 2) $$X = \sum_{k=0}^5 t_k \cdot 3^k, \qquad \text{where } 3^6 = 729 \ge 256$$

Each successive nucleotide is selected deterministically: $B_k = \mathcal{A}(B_{k-1})[t_k]$. Because $3^6 = 729 \ge 256$, the encoding map $\phi: \{0, \dots, 255\} \to \mathcal{B}^6$ is strictly injective. Inversion is exact:

(Eq. 3) $$t_k = \text{index}\big(B_k, \mathcal{A}(B_{k-1})\big), \qquad X = \sum_{k=0}^5 t_k \cdot 3^k \pmod{256}$$
Homopolymer Run Guarantee:
$$\max_i \{\text{run}_i\} = 1 \quad (\text{Zero adjacent repeated nucleotides})$$ Synthesizability is 100% guaranteed across all oligo sequences.
Equilibrium GC Content:
$$\text{GC-Ratio} = \frac{N_G + N_C}{N_{\text{total}}} \times 100\% \approx 50.0\% \in [45\%, 55\%]$$ Prevents secondary hairpin folding and stabilizes duplex melting ($T_m$).

2.2 Classical Reed-Solomon Codebook ($GF(2^8)$) & Failure Mechanics

The industry-standard baseline encodes payload blocks into RS codewords over Galois Field $GF(2^8)$ with generator polynomial $g(x) = \prod_{j=1}^{2t} (x - \alpha^j)$. At the receiver, syndromes are evaluated:

(Eq. 4) $$S_j = r(\alpha^j) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} r_i (\alpha^j)^i, \qquad j = 1, \dots, 2t$$
The Root Failure Mechanism: Coordinate Frame Drift
Reed-Solomon assumes that received symbol $r_i$ corresponds to spatial position $c_i$. Under an unmarked burst deletion of $b$ nucleotides in the DNA strand ($y = (c_0, \dots, c_{s-1}, c_{s+b}, \dots, c_{M-1})$):
  1. The trit stream is shifted by $b$ positions. The byte parser groups out-of-phase trits (Byte $k$ absorbs trits from Byte $k+1$).
  2. Symbol coordinates drift by $\Delta i = -\lfloor b / 6 \rfloor$. The syndrome evaluates across shifted bases: $S_j' = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} c_{i + \Delta i} (\alpha^j)^i \ne \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} e_i (\alpha^j)^i$.
  3. The Berlekamp-Massey algorithm cannot find roots for locator $\Lambda(x)$ in $GF(2^8)$, declaring uncorrectable error across the entire remaining strand.

2.3 Generalized Patha Code (GPC) Formulation & Synchronization

Generalized Patha Codes resolve Coordinate Frame Drift without expensive matrix dynamic programming. GPC chunks the 1,024-bit input stream into $K=4$ binary nibbles and applies Toroidal Stage-Major Windowing interspersed with deterministic pilot anchors ($p_k = 1$):

(Eq. 5) $$c_i = \begin{cases} p_0 = 1, & \text{if } \pi(i) = 0 \quad (\text{Pilot Anchor}) \\ x_{\pi(i)}, & \text{if } \pi(i) \in \{1, \dots, K\} \quad (\text{Payload Bit}) \end{cases} \qquad \text{Block Length: } M = 13K + 6 = 58 \text{ bits}$$

Pilot anchors are deterministically placed at coordinate indices: $\mathcal{P} = \{0, 2K+1, 4K+2, 7K+3, 10K+4, 13K+5\} = \{0, 9, 18, 31, 44, 57\}$.

Linear-Time Greedy Sliding-Window Alignment Algorithm

When an enzymatic burst deletion of length $b$ strikes, the received chunk length is compressed to $M' = M - b_{\text{bits}}$. Rather than computing an $\mathcal{O}(M^2)$ Needleman-Wunsch edit distance matrix, GPC evaluates pilot anchor correlation over candidate displacement hypotheses $d \in [0, b_{\max}]$:

(Eq. 6) $$d^* = \arg\max_{d \in [0, b_{\max}]} \sum_{k=0}^5 \mathbb{I}\big(y_{p_k - d} = 1\big)$$

Once optimal displacement $d^*$ is locked, the coordinate map is realigned, and each source bit $j \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ is decoded via non-destructive majority voting over its surviving support set $S_j \setminus E$:

(Eq. 7) $$\hat{x}_j = \arg\max_{v \in \{0, 1\}} \sum_{i \in \text{unravel}(S_j, d^*)} \mathbb{I}(y_i = v) \qquad \text{Algorithmic Complexity: } \mathcal{O}(6 \cdot b_{\max} + M) = \mathcal{O}(M)$$

Why RS Cannot Be Saved by Adding More Parity: In classical RS codes, adding parity symbols increases error tolerance over a fixed grid. But when an indel shifts the grid, the parity symbols themselves are sampled at out-of-phase coordinates. Adding 50% more parity yields zero improvement against coordinate drift.

The Physics of GPC's Breaking Boundary ($b > 20\text{ nt}$): In GPC, each symbol $j \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ has an unraveling span $\text{span}_j = \max(S_j) - \min(S_j) \ge 47\text{ bits}$. When burst deletion length $b_{\text{bits}} > 40\text{ bits}$ ($b > 20\text{ nt}$), the deletion window exceeds the minimum span between redundant copies of the minority symbol. At that point, $|S_{j^*} \setminus E| = 0$, forcing the decoder to make an unassisted guess, producing graceful degradation ($7.71\%$ at $b=25\text{ nt}$).
